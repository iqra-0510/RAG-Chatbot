Study Strategies and Exam Preparation

Active recall means testing yourself instead of only rereading notes. Close the book and try to write or say what you remember, then check your answers. Spaced repetition means reviewing a topic again after increasing gaps of time, such as after one day, three days and one week. Both methods improve long term memory.

The Pomodoro technique divides study time into focused sessions of 25 minutes, followed by a short break of 5 minutes. After four sessions, take a longer break of 15 to 30 minutes. This helps to avoid tiredness and keeps concentration high.

To make a study timetable, list all subjects and exam dates, give more time to difficult subjects, and keep short breaks between sessions. Revise each subject a little every day rather than leaving everything for the last night.

Practice past-style questions and solve numerical problems by hand. Explaining a concept in simple words to a friend is a good way to check whether you really understand it. Getting enough sleep before an exam helps memory and concentration.

When preparing an academic presentation, start with a clear title and objective, keep each slide focused on one idea, use short points instead of long paragraphs, and finish with a summary slide.
